Research

Mathematical neurobiology across scales

The brain is a network of neurons embedded in a living biological environment. Chemical signals, metabolism, and interactions with surrounding cells influence neuronal activity, while neuronal activity changes these processes in return. I study how these interactions shape brain function and disease.

My research combines dynamical systems, network science, and statistical inference with neuroimaging and experimental data. I develop models that connect biological mechanisms across scales and investigate what we can learn about those mechanisms from observations of the brain.

Research program

Brain function and disease

Neurodegenerative disease

How do protein transport, cellular vulnerability, and neuronal activity interact to determine where disease begins and how it progresses? I develop mathematical models linking these processes to experimental observations and human neuroimaging.

Brain dynamics across scales

How can we connect the activity of individual neurons to the collective dynamics measured by brain imaging? Building on my work on mean-field models, I aim to understand which dynamics can be preserved across spatial scales and which biological mechanisms remain identifiable.

Flexible cognition and computation

How can the same neural circuit support different functional states? I aim to investigate whether interactions with slower biological processes allow networks to switch between activity patterns and perform different computations without changing their connections.

Background

Training across mathematics, biology, and complex systems

I am currently a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Complex Systems Lab at the Wu Tsai Institute, Yale University, led by Prof. Dani Bassett.

My research draws on training in computational biology, mathematics, and complex systems. I bring these perspectives together to connect biological mechanisms with collective brain dynamics, working with experimental and neuroimaging data to develop and test mathematical models.

Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher in Bioengineering at the University of Pennsylvania from 2024 to 2026, under the mentorship of Prof. Dani Bassett. I completed my PhD in Mathematics at the University of Oxford under Prof. Alain Goriely and Prof. Christian Bick, funded by Aker Scholarship. Before that, I completed my MSc in Computational Biology at NTNU under Prof. Eivind Almaas.
